# Pharmacy and pharmaceutical specialist skills for digital and AI-enabled practice

The goal is to become a **pharmacist who can analyze medicines, medication-use systems, pharmaceutical products and operations with data and AI**—not an accountant who happens to use spreadsheets.

## Core pharmacy informatics
1. Medicine master data: generic/brand, active ingredient, strength, dosage form, route, release type, concentration, pack size, manufacturer and registration identifiers.
2. Pharmaceutical units: mg, mcg, g, mL, units, IU, concentration, pack-to-unit conversion and dimensional consistency.
3. Batch traceability: lot/batch, expiry, supplier, storage conditions, quarantine, recalls and FEFO.
4. Medication-use workflow: procurement, receiving, storage, prescribing interface, dispensing, counseling, returns and recall handling.
5. Medication safety: LASA medicines, high-alert medicines, look-alike packaging, cold chain and controlled documentation.
6. Data quality: terminology normalization, duplicate detection, provenance, timestamps, audit trails and validation rules.

## Inventory analytics
- Consumption vs issues vs demand; stockout-censored demand.
- Average daily/monthly use, seasonality and lead-time variability.
- Minimum/maximum, reorder point, safety stock and service-level trade-offs.
- ABC (financial usage value), VEN (clinical criticality), ABC-VEN matrix.
- FSN movement, XYZ variability, FEFO and expiry exposure.
- Formulary and essential-medicine availability indicators.
- Forecast baselines, forecast error (MAE, RMSE, MAPE caveats) and backtesting.
- Cold-chain and batch-level traceability; no silent merging of strengths or dosage forms.

## Pharmaceutical manufacturing and industrial pharmacy
- API/drug substance vs finished drug product; excipient functions and dosage-form families.
- Manufacturing process maps and unit-operation concepts for solid, liquid, semi-solid and sterile products.
- GMP systems: materials, premises, equipment, documentation, data integrity and supplier controls.
- Deviations, root-cause analysis, CAPA, change control, qualification and validation concepts.
- Material/batch genealogy, yield reconciliation, in-process controls and continued process monitoring.
- Packaging, storage, cold chain, complaints, recalls and product lifecycle.

## Pharmaceutical development, QbD and CMC
- TPP and QTPP; CQA, CMA, CPP and product/process understanding.
- Quality risk management, FMEA, DoE, control strategy and lifecycle knowledge management.
- Preformulation, excipient compatibility, analytical development, dissolution and stability concepts.
- API and finished-product CMC concepts, scale-up, technology transfer and dossier structure.
- Distinguish development observations from specifications, validated ranges and release criteria.

## Drug discovery and R&D
- Disease biology, target identification/validation, assays, screening and hit confirmation.
- Hit-to-lead, lead optimization, medicinal chemistry and structure–activity relationships (SAR).
- ADME/DMPK, exposure, metabolism, safety pharmacology and preclinical evidence.
- Clinical-development phases, endpoints, regulatory review and post-market evidence.
- Public chemical/bioactivity data, cheminformatics, molecular descriptors, QSAR and docking limitations.
- Reproducible evidence mapping, provenance, negative results and model applicability domain.

## Pharmacovigilance and medication safety
- ICSR concepts, seriousness, expectedness, MedDRA vocabulary, duplicate reports and case narratives.
- FAERS/openFDA limitations, disproportionality methods (ROR/PRR) and confidence intervals.
- Signal detection is hypothesis-generating, not causality or incidence.
- Medication error taxonomy, LASA and risk controls.

## AI and digital health
- Structured extraction with schema validation and human confirmation.
- RAG over approved drug/quality documents with source citations and abstention.
- Classification and forecasting with temporal validation, leakage checks and calibration.
- Workflow automation with role-based access, audit logs, approvals and fail-safe design.
- Model governance: intended use, limitations, bias, monitoring, versioning and change control.
- Cheminformatics/ML workflows with curated labels, scaffold-aware validation and applicability-domain reporting.

## Practical assessment

For each medicine or product-development project, demonstrate correct ingredient/strength/form/route and unit handling, batch/material traceability, source provenance, scientific rationale, quality/clinical review boundaries, reproducibility and a written limitations section. Synthetic training scores must never be presented as approved specifications or operational limits.
