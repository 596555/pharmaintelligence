# Pharmacy specialist skills for digital and AI-enabled practice

The goal is to become a **pharmacist who can analyze medicines, medication-use systems and pharmacy operations with data and AI**, not an accountant who happens to use spreadsheets.

## Core pharmacy informatics
1. Medicine master data: generic/brand, active ingredient, strength, dosage form, route, release type, concentration, pack size, manufacturer and registration identifiers.
2. Pharmaceutical units: mg, mcg, g, mL, units, IU, concentration, pack-to-unit conversion and dimensional consistency.
3. Batch traceability: lot/batch, expiry, supplier, storage conditions, quarantine, recalls and FEFO.
4. Medication-use workflow: procurement, receiving, storage, prescribing interface, dispensing, counseling, returns and recall handling.
5. Medication safety: LASA medicines, high-alert medicines, look-alike packaging, cold chain and controlled documentation.
6. Data quality: terminology normalization, duplicate detection, provenance, timestamps, audit trails and validation rules.

## Inventory analytics
- Consumption vs issues vs demand; stockout-censored demand.
- Average daily/monthly use, seasonality and lead-time variability.
- Minimum/maximum, reorder point, safety stock and service-level trade-offs.
- ABC (financial usage value), VEN (clinical criticality), ABC-VEN matrix.
- FSN movement, XYZ variability, FEFO and expiry exposure.
- Formulary and essential-medicine availability indicators.
- Forecast baselines, forecast error (MAE, RMSE, MAPE caveats) and backtesting.
- Cold-chain and batch-level traceability; no silent merging of strengths or dosage forms.

## Pharmacovigilance and medication safety
- ICSR concepts, seriousness, expectedness, MedDRA vocabulary, duplicate reports and case narratives.
- FAERS/openFDA limitations, disproportionality methods (ROR/PRR) and confidence intervals.
- Signal detection is hypothesis-generating, not causality or incidence.
- Medication error taxonomy, LASA and risk controls.

## AI and digital health
- Structured extraction with schema validation and human confirmation.
- RAG over approved drug information with source citations and abstention.
- Classification and forecasting with temporal validation, leakage checks and calibration.
- Workflow automation with role-based access, audit logs, approvals and fail-safe design.
- Model governance: intended use, limitations, bias, monitoring, versioning and change control.

## Practical assessment
For each medicine-related project, demonstrate correct ingredient/strength/form/route handling, units, source provenance, clinical review boundaries, reproducibility and a written limitations section.
