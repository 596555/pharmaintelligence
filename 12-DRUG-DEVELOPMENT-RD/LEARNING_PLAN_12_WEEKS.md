# 12-week learning plan — Manufacturing, R&D and Drug Discovery

**Recommended effort:** 10 hours/week for 12 weeks (about 120 hours), integrated into the wider 365-day programme. This is a specialization block, not a replacement for Python, SQL, statistics, BI and pharmacy analytics.

| Week | Learning focus | Practical output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pharmaceutical industry map; API, excipients, intermediates, finished products; dosage forms and routes | Medicine-product lifecycle map |
| 2 | Manufacturing flow and unit operations: dispensing, mixing, granulation, drying, milling, compression, coating, filling and packaging | Process map for one dosage form |
| 3 | GMP, documentation, data integrity, deviations, CAPA, change control, qualification and validation vocabulary | GMP record-review checklist |
| 4 | Quality systems, contamination control, cleaning, environmental monitoring, utilities and warehouse controls | Risk map for a fictional manufacturing site |
| 5 | Preformulation, solubility, pKa, polymorphism, particle size, excipient compatibility and stability concepts | Preformulation evidence table |
| 6 | QbD: QTPP, CQAs, CMAs, CPPs, risk assessment and control strategy | QTPP/CQA/CPP map for a fictional immediate-release tablet |
| 7 | Analytical development, method validation concepts, specifications, dissolution and stability studies | Synthetic analytical dataset with a data dictionary |
| 8 | CMC, API vs finished product, scale-up, technology transfer and lifecycle management | Fictional CMC module outline |
| 9 | Discovery: disease biology, target identification/validation, assays, screening and hit confirmation | Target evidence matrix |
| 10 | Hit-to-lead, lead optimization, SAR, ADME/DMPK, safety pharmacology and preclinical evidence | Candidate comparison worksheet using public/teaching data |
| 11 | Clinical-development phases, trial endpoints, regulatory review and post-market lifecycle | Development-stage evidence map |
| 12 | Cheminformatics/AI concepts and integration into the Workbench | Reproducible mini case study and documented limitations |

## Suggested weekly allocation

- 3 hours: reading and structured notes.
- 4 hours: practical data, process maps or code.
- 2 hours: interpret the scientific/pharmaceutical meaning.
- 1 hour: test, document and publish.

## Completion criteria

A week is complete only when the output is reproducible and you can explain:
- Which pharmaceutical question it answers.
- Which data and assumptions it uses.
- What a pharmacist, formulation scientist, process engineer or quality professional must review.
- What the analysis cannot conclude.

## Free study sources

Start with the official ICH quality guidelines (Q8, Q9, Q10, Q11, Q12, Q13 and Q14), WHO GMP material, FDA drug-development and process-validation resources, and the public chemical/bioactivity resources linked in [the main track README](README.md). Read guidance documents in context; a guideline summary is not a substitute for the current applicable text or local regulation.
