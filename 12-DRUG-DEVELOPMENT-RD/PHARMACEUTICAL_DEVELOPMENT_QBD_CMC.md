# Pharmaceutical development, Quality by Design and CMC

## 1. Begin with the intended product

The **Target Product Profile (TPP)** expresses the intended clinical/product concept. The **Quality Target Product Profile (QTPP)** translates relevant product objectives into quality-related targets such as dosage form, route, strength, release characteristics, container closure and stability profile.

A QTPP is a development tool, not a marketing claim or a substitute for product-specific evidence.

## 2. Core QbD concepts

- **CQA — Critical Quality Attribute:** a physical, chemical, biological or microbiological property that should be within an appropriate limit, range or distribution to help assure desired product quality.
- **CMA — Critical Material Attribute:** a material property that may affect a CQA.
- **CPP — Critical Process Parameter:** a process parameter whose variability can affect a CQA and therefore should be monitored or controlled.
- **Risk assessment:** a structured, science-based process to identify, analyze and evaluate quality risks.
- **Design of Experiments (DoE):** a planned experimental approach to understand relationships between factors and responses.
- **Control strategy:** planned controls derived from product/process understanding to assure consistent quality.
- **Design space:** a multidimensional combination/interaction of input variables and process parameters demonstrated to provide assurance of quality; it is not a guessed operating range.

Potential CQA examples depend on product type: assay, content uniformity, dissolution/release, impurities/degradation products, water content, microbial attributes, particle-size distribution, sterility-related attributes or container-closure performance. Not every attribute applies to every product.

## 3. A defensible development workflow

1. Define the intended use and QTPP.
2. Identify candidate CQAs using product knowledge and scientific rationale.
3. Identify material attributes and process parameters that could affect those CQAs.
4. Document uncertainties and perform proportionate quality-risk assessment.
5. Design studies/experiments to generate evidence; retain raw data and protocol context.
6. Analyze main effects, interactions, variability and model assumptions.
7. Develop a control strategy and explain how it will be verified.
8. Confirm stability, scale-up and transfer considerations.
9. Maintain knowledge and change control throughout the product lifecycle.

An FMEA score or RPN can help organize review; it cannot independently decide that a risk is acceptable. Severity, occurrence and detectability scales must be defined for the specific context and reviewed by qualified people.

## 4. Analytical development and stability

Build literacy in:
- Analytical target profile and intended analytical use.
- Specificity/selectivity, accuracy, precision, range, linearity, detection/quantitation limits and robustness where applicable.
- Reference standards, system suitability, sample handling and method version control.
- Dissolution/release testing and interpretation within a validated method and product context.
- Stability-indicating methods, storage conditions, timepoints, packaging and trend evaluation.
- ICH stability, analytical validation and analytical procedure development guidance (Q1, Q2 and Q14).

Do not create your own release limits from a teaching example. Specifications are product- and jurisdiction-specific and require justified, approved documentation.

## 5. CMC and the product dossier

CMC means Chemistry, Manufacturing and Controls. Learn to distinguish:
- Drug-substance information (for example, starting materials, manufacturing process, characterization, controls and stability).
- Drug-product information (composition, pharmaceutical development, manufacturing process, excipient controls, finished-product controls, container closure and stability).
- How development knowledge supports process understanding, control strategy, technology transfer and lifecycle changes.

For orientation, the common CTD quality structure includes Module 3 sections for drug substance (3.2.S) and drug product (3.2.P). The dossier structure and requirements vary with product and regulator; consult the applicable current guidance.

## 6. Key ICH guidance families

- Q8: Pharmaceutical Development.
- Q9(R1): Quality Risk Management.
- Q10: Pharmaceutical Quality System.
- Q11: Development and Manufacture of Drug Substances.
- Q12: Product Lifecycle Management.
- Q13: Continuous Manufacturing.
- Q14: Analytical Procedure Development; read with Q2 for analytical validation.
