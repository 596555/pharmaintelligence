# Pharmaceutical manufacturing and GMP

## 1. Understand the product and manufacturing chain

A medicine may involve:
- **Drug substance / API:** the active pharmaceutical ingredient.
- **Excipients:** formulation components with defined functional roles.
- **Drug product:** the finished dosage form containing the API and excipients.
- **Primary packaging:** packaging in direct contact with the dosage form.
- **Secondary packaging and labeling:** carton, leaflet, serialization or other market-specific information.

A simplified lifecycle is: supplier qualification and material receipt → sampling/testing/release by Quality → dispensing/weighing → manufacturing operations → in-process controls → packaging and labeling → finished-product testing and Quality disposition → storage/distribution → complaint, recall and lifecycle monitoring. The exact flow depends on the product, site, technology and applicable regulation.

## 2. Dosage forms and common unit operations

| Product family | Typical operations to understand |
|---|---|
| Immediate-release tablets | Dispensing, blending, granulation when used, drying, milling, lubrication, compression, coating and packaging |
| Hard/soft capsules | Material preparation, blend or fill preparation, encapsulation, inspection and packaging |
| Oral liquids | Water/vehicle preparation, dissolution or dispersion, mixing, filtration where appropriate, filling and closure |
| Semisolids | Phase preparation, emulsification or mixing, homogenization, deaeration, filling and packaging |
| Sterile products | Specialized compounding, bioburden/endotoxin controls, sterilization or aseptic processing, environmental monitoring, container closure integrity and qualified facilities |
| Modified-release products | Formulation and process controls linked to release mechanism, performance testing and stability |

Do not transfer process parameters from one formulation or site to another. A process map is a learning artifact, not an operating instruction.

## 3. GMP and pharmaceutical quality systems

Learn the purpose and relationship of:
- Personnel training, hygiene and responsibilities.
- Premises, equipment, utilities and maintenance.
- Materials, supplier controls, sampling and status labeling.
- Master production instructions and contemporaneous batch records.
- In-process controls, laboratory controls and out-of-specification investigations.
- Deviations, root-cause analysis, CAPA and change control.
- Data integrity, audit trails, access controls and record retention.
- Complaints, returns, recalls, product quality review and ongoing monitoring.
- Qualification, equipment calibration, cleaning validation and process validation.

GMP is a system of controlled people, premises, processes, records and oversight—not a checklist or a spreadsheet exercise.

## 4. Validation and lifecycle thinking

Study qualification and validation as distinct but connected concepts:
- User requirements and design qualification where applicable.
- Installation, operational and performance qualification.
- Process design, process qualification and continued process verification.
- Cleaning validation and analytical method validation.
- Technology transfer and comparability/change assessment.

FDA's process-validation guidance describes a lifecycle approach. Apply the current guidance and local requirements rather than treating a one-time successful batch as proof of a validated process.

## 5. Useful manufacturing data skills

Learn to model:
- Material genealogy: supplier, material code, lot, retest/expiry and disposition.
- Batch genealogy: product, batch, equipment, process stage, time, operator role and linked records.
- Process parameters: CPP candidate, measured value, unit, time, equipment and source record.
- Quality attributes: test, method/version, sample point, result, unit and specification version.
- Deviations/CAPA: category, severity rationale, cause evidence, action owner, due date and effectiveness check.
- Stability: condition, timepoint, packaging configuration, method and trend.

Potential analytics include yield reconciliation, right-first-time indicators, deviation trends, process capability (only when assumptions are met), stability trending and supplier/material performance. Each metric needs a documented denominator, data lineage and context.

## 6. Do not do this

- Do not invent or copy manufacturing parameters as instructions.
- Do not use a synthetic dataset to set a real specification or acceptance limit.
- Do not automatically decide a batch is releasable.
- Do not equate an SPC signal with proof of a root cause.
- Do not edit or reconstruct GMP records; preserve controlled records and audit trails.
- Do not treat a risk score as a substitute for scientific review.
